Google Analytics 4: What You Need to Know

By Anil Sharma · Updated 27 November 2026

Google Analytics 4 (GA4) is Google's analytics platform for websites and apps. It replaced Universal Analytics and measures things differently. Here are the parts that matter most for businesses.

User-centric measurement

GA4 is built around users and events rather than sessions and pageviews. That makes it easier to see how the same person engages with you over time and across devices.

Everything is an event

Clicks, scrolls, video plays, form submissions and purchases are all events. Many are tracked automatically with enhanced measurement, and the rest can be added through Google Tag Manager. Mark the important ones as key events (conversions).

Cross-platform tracking

One GA4 property can collect data from your website and apps, giving a single view of the customer journey.

Machine learning and predictive metrics

With enough data, GA4 predicts purchase probability and churn, and surfaces unusual changes automatically. These predictive audiences can be shared with Google Ads.

Privacy and consent

GA4 offers more control over data collection and retention, and works with Consent Mode, which is now essential for advertisers in the EU and UK.

Connect it to Google Ads and Search Console

Linking GA4 with Google Ads and Search Console brings search queries, ad costs and conversions into one place, and lets Google Ads bid using your GA4 key events.
